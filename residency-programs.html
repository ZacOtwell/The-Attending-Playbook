Why this matters

The financial learning curve gets steep right when clinical training is ending.

Residents and fellows can go from a relatively simple financial life to a six-figure income, a new benefits package, major debt decisions, insurance needs, taxes, and a home purchase in a matter of months.

Most trainees are making those decisions while interviewing, moving, finishing boards, and preparing for a new job. The information is available, but it is scattered — and generic personal finance advice rarely accounts for physician-specific issues.

The Attending Playbook is designed to give programs a practical way to introduce the right financial topics before those decisions become urgent.

What trainees leave with

A clearer sequence for the decisions ahead.

The goal is not to turn residents into financial experts. It is to help them recognize the major decisions, understand the tradeoffs, and know which ones deserve attention first.

Know what is urgentSeparate decisions that need attention before graduation from those that can wait until attending life settles down.
Ask better questionsUnderstand the terminology and tradeoffs well enough to evaluate benefits, loans, insurance, and outside advice.
Avoid expensive defaultsCatch common mistakes around withholding, PSLF, coverage gaps, home affordability, and retirement elections early.
Have a next-step checklistLeave with concrete actions instead of a pile of concepts that are hard to apply after the session.
